# Sustainable Debt: Bond Pricing and Loan Economics
### ShangyuZ · BSc Statistics, Economics & Finance · UCL

An independent research project on one financing question: **how are
sustainability features reflected in the price of debt?** It looks at the
question from the bond side, with a matched-pair pricing study, and from the loan
side, with an illustrative structuring model. A descriptive issuance analysis and
a climate-data dashboard support both.

## The question

Labelled debt makes one of two kinds of promise. **Use-of-proceeds** instruments
(green bonds) promise what the money is spent on; any pricing effect comes from
investors. **Performance-linked** instruments (sustainability-linked loans and
bonds) promise an *outcome*, and the borrower's margin moves with whether it is
achieved. Each component below asks what the evidence, or the arithmetic, says
about the size of that pricing effect.

## The work and the evidence

| Component | What it is | The defensible result |
|---|---|---|
| **[Bond pricing](./project1-green-bond-analysis/)** | Nine German green Federal securities compared with their conventional twins — same issuer, coupon and maturity | The green bonds yield **1.50bps less** on average across 8,094 paired daily observations (Sep 2020 – Oct 2026). The sign holds under every inference choice tested; the precision is modest (t = −4.5 with panel-robust standard errors). |
| **[Issuance sample](./project1-green-bond-analysis/)** | Descriptive analysis of 732 labelled bonds from the Climate Bonds Initiative's News Makers extract | In this sample, deal count grew 2,280% from 2018 to 2023 but dollar volume grew 950%, because average deal size fell. |
| **[Loan economics](./project2-sll-structuring/)** | An illustrative £650m sustainability-linked revolver for a fictional borrower, with a formula-driven Excel model and a structuring memo | The best-case net benefit of the sustainability feature, compared with an equivalent conventional revolver, is **£192.5k a year at 60% utilisation**. It disappears below about **20.5%**, under the model's assumptions. |
| **[Climate data dashboard](./project3-climate-dashboard/)** | Streamlit app on Our World in Data energy and CO₂ data, with an illustrative portfolio carbon-intensity calculator | Supporting context; not a source of pricing evidence. |

## Limitations

- **The bond result is German sovereign only, and secondary-market.** It shows
  how existing green twins are priced relative to conventional ones. It is not a
  measured funding saving on a new issue, and it does not transfer to corporate
  issuers. Liquidity and repo differences between the twins remain possible
  contributors.
- **The issuance data is a curated newsflow sample**, not a census. Its 82%
  sovereign share sits beside about 11.6% in CBI's market-wide summary, so its
  composition cannot stand in for the market's.
- **The loan model is a scenario.** The borrower, targets and costs are
  illustrative; utilisation is assumed, not forecast; and the targets' alignment
  with SBTi or other frameworks has not been independently established.
- **The dashboard's carbon calculator uses assumed sector intensities**, and its
  EU carbon price series is indicative and uncited.

## Implementation

- **Python** (pandas, NumPy) for cleaning, aggregation and the greenium
  estimator, including Driscoll-Kraay panel standard errors.
- **Excel** models generated by script (openpyxl), with the formulas evaluated in
  the test suite and compared with the Python calculations.
- **Tests and CI** check key calculations and the consistency of published
  figures with the committed data. They do not validate every interpretation or
  citation.

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
pytest tests/

python project1-green-bond-analysis/scripts/process_data.py    # rebuild the issuance workbook
python project2-sll-structuring/scripts/build_model.py         # rebuild the SLL workbook
cd project3-climate-dashboard && streamlit run app.py          # run the dashboard
```

## Data and provenance

Every source is publicly accessible without a paid subscription; their terms
differ. Two publishers reserve their rights, so their records are not in this
repository — only derived output is. Full detail in **[DATA.md](./DATA.md)**.

| Dataset | In this repository | Terms |
|---------|--------------------|-------|
| Green bond issuance | Derived aggregates only | Climate Bonds Initiative — all rights reserved |
| German Federal securities yields | Estimator output only | Deutsche Finanzagentur — all rights reserved; refetch with `scripts/fetch_bund_yields.py` |
| Energy and CO₂ by country | Fetched at runtime | Our World in Data — CC BY 4.0 |
| EU ETS carbon prices | Bundled CSV | Uncited; indicative only |
| Sector carbon intensities | Hard-coded | Assumed illustrative inputs |

## How this was built, and how it was checked

This project was built with substantial help from Claude (via Claude Code); the
commit history records it. I chose the questions and the methods, made the
judgement calls, and checked the results. The conclusions are mine to defend.

The work has been through my own audit and two outside reviews. Each found real
errors — in the inference behind the bond result, in market-wide claims the
issuance sample could not support, in citations, and in the loan memo's
interpretation. **[FINDINGS.md](./FINDINGS.md)** records what was wrong and what
changed, and **[CHANGELOG.md](./CHANGELOG.md)** has the version history.
